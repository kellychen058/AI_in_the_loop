Problem Grounding 
Three questions:
Who specifically has the collaboration problem you are addressing? Name a real type of person, not a generic "user."
Product managers, designers, and engineers on small product teams
What do they currently do instead of your tool?
messaging app + calender
What would be observably different about their collaboration if your tool worked?
Reduce the time and cost associated with communication and content errors in message transmission and rescheduling tasks. Avoid discussing proposals that have previously been rejected.
Evaluation Plan Draft 
Four fields:
Success definition — We will know our tool works if it identifies at least 80% of known individual scheduling conflicts in test plans, and teams take at least 25% less time to produce an agreed task schedule than they do with their current process. The tool must not change anyone’s schedule without approval.
Target users — Product managers, designers, and engineers on two small product teams (approximately 8–12 participants total). We will recruit through product teams we can contact directly and ask participants to use a sample or consented copy of their task and availability data.
Method — First, observe each team scheduling a set of representative tasks using its current tools. Then, during a one-week pilot, have the same participants plan comparable tasks with our tool. Record conflicts identified, missed conflicts, false alerts, time to an agreed schedule, and changes made after teammates review it. Follow up with short interviews about suggestions they accepted or rejected.
Minimum evidence threshold — A skeptical reader should see the task sets, how we determined which conflicts were real, and results for both teams—not just favorable examples. We would look for the conflict-detection and time targets above, with no unapproved schedule changes. If the sample is too small or the tasks are not comparable, we will report the findings as preliminary rather than claim the tool is proven.
Human–AI boundary — The AI may detect overlaps and workload conflicts, explain why it flagged them, and propose task times. Team members decide task priority, whether a deadline can move, and whether to accept a proposed change. The tool makes no calendar or task-board edits until all affected people approve them.
